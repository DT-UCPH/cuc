# Reconciling expert feedback

Establish the exact review basis and preserve the original comments before
repairing delimiters, physical newlines, or overwritten sign spans. The current
reviewed format has eight fields; token identity, line references and sign spans
are evidence, not editable analysis. Validate structure before scoring.

Classify each remark separately: correction, explicit alternative, rejection,
question, or proposal to change notation. “Not necessarily D” does not assert G;
“could this be an infinitive?” does not select one. Do not implement speculative
notation changes while repairing a file. Resolve linguistic interpretation and
its project encoding as separate decisions.

Follow the user's requested ordering of expert readings; for the KTU 1.5 Tania
reconciliation her explicit option goes first. Keep independently sourced
conflicting readings below with precise attribution. If she only rejects an
option, do not label a remaining option as her positive judgment. Keep verbatim
feedback in the audit record; attribute paraphrases in the reviewed comments.

Read the actual source's grammatical category. An etymology is not a synchronic
participle, a translation is not a stem label, and `Not in DULAT` is not itself
evidence for an alternative. Conversely, DULAT absence cannot veto a reading
explicitly supported by an expert or another source. Preserve a source's stated
uncertainty. If an encoding is invalid, retain the scholarly proposal in an
attributed note until it can be represented correctly; do not keep invalid rows
as accepted gold alternatives. Archive removed hypotheses and reasons.

KTU 1.5 examples: Wyatt (2002), pp. 118–119 n. 20, explicitly proposes D `imt`
although Tania rejects it. Tropper (2012), p. 602, treats `šlyṭ` as a divine name
with tentative verbal-adjective etymologies. DULAT p. 810, entry 4055,
explicitly reports De Moor’s ly/uṭ Š proposal and Margalit’s lw/yṭ derivation.
Read full source notes, including dissent and other etymologies: structured
headword/form fields omit some published options. Do not call a proposal invented
because it lacks a standalone DULAT headword. The exact active/passive participial
encodings formerly entered by the review still require source-by-source checking. DULAT s.v. /n-š-y/ records dissenting readings
of `nšt` at I:26; its N reading is only one option.

Recheck every retained alternative in the clause, including case and agreement.
A default nominative or inherited construct state is not syntactic evidence.
Inspect existing warnings as well as errors: the missing infinitive `[/` in
KTU 1.5 I:26 was already detectable as a warning. Record dispositions instead of
adding duplicate rules or suppressing source disagreements with broad allowlists.
