# Ugaritic Gt-Stem Encoding

This reference operationalizes Chapter 6 of Tania Notarius's draft monograph (`agent/local_sources/notarius.compact.html`). The local Wikipedia snapshot supplies the traditional shorthand “simple reflexive” and the templates `ʼirtagama` / `yartagamu`; use it only as a compact paradigm cross-check. Chapter 6 demonstrates that “reflexive” alone is too narrow.

## Evidence Boundary

Treat these claims as deterministic enough for tooling:

- Gt is a G-derived stem whose infixed `-t-` is explicit in most secure forms (§§6.1.1, 6.2.1).
- I-s/ṣ/z and I-t/ṭ/d roots can make G and Gt indistinguishable through assimilation; surface shape alone cannot decide them (§6.2.1).
- Gt is predominantly finite and prefix-conjugated. Imperatives and verbal nouns are rare; a participle is virtually unattested (§6.2.1). Use this distribution as a review warning, not an impossibility rule.
- I-n roots are well represented. When `/n/` is elided before the formative, reconstruct the lexical radical before `]t]`.

Do not make these semantic generalizations hard parser rules:

- The principal functions are direct reflexive, indirect reflexive, reciprocal, autobenefactive/intensifying, and denominative.
- Rare non-agentive resultant or anticausative uses occur, especially in prose.
- Most forms are agentive and dynamic, but transitivity varies by semantic subtype.
- Genre and conjugation distributions are tendencies, not token-level proofs.

## Marker Semantics and Order

- `]t]`: written Gt formative.
- `(x`: reconstructed lexical radical `x` absent from the surface.
- `&x`: written sign `x` not present in the lexical representation.
- `!x!`: prefix-conjugation preformative.
- `[`: verbal root boundary.

Assimilated I-dental forms expose an unresolved notation issue. The reviewed corpus currently uses an assimilated marker such as `]ṯ]` in the uncertain `yṯṯbn` analysis. Do not replace it with written `]t]`, and do not establish a new hidden/allomorphic marker convention from one disputed form. Retain `Gt?` until the linguistic reading and the project notation are agreed together.

The analysis follows lexical order, including reconstructed radicals:

| Surface | Root | Canonical analysis | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ttpl` | `/n-p-l/` | `!t!(n]t]pl[` | preformative `t`, elided lexical `n`, written Gt `t` |
| `ttpp` | `/n-p(-p)/` | `!t!(n]t]pp[` | same I-n ordering |
| `ytlk` | `/h-l-k/` | `!y!(h]t]lk[` | elided lexical `h` precedes the infix structurally |
| `yitmr` | `/ʔ-m-r/` | `!y!(ʔ&i]t]mr[` | lexical aleph corresponds to written `i` before the infix |
| `its` | `/n-s(-y)/ (I)` | `!(ʔ&i!(n]t]s(y(I)[` | 1cs `i`, elided I-n, infix, weak III-y |
| `itdb` | `/ʔ-b-d/` | `(ʔ&i]t]db[` | exceptional written metathesis; retain an explanatory comment |

Reject these structural near misses:

- `!t!](n]tpl[`: malformed marker and no explicit Gt formative.
- `!t!]t](hlk[`: reconstructs the surface but reverses the historical order of `/h/` and the infix.
- `!y!(ʔ&itmr[`: conflates the written `i` with the Gt formative.
- `!(ʔ&i!(nts(I)[`: reconstructs the surface but leaves the Gt `t` untyped.

Surface reconstruction is necessary but insufficient: also confirm the root, stem, conjugation, and agreement.

## Attested Semantic Inventory

Use this inventory to search and compare parallels, not as a whitelist that overrides DULAT or context.

- Direct reflexive/body action: `n-ġ-r`, `n-p(-p)`, `p-r-ʕ`, `r-ḥ-ṣ`, `n-š-ʔ`.
- Indirect reflexive/possession: `ʔ-s-p`, `p-q`, `š-k-n`, `y-r-ṯ`.
- Reciprocal fighting: `ḫ-ṣ-b`, `m-ḫ-ṣ`.
- Autobenefactive, intensified, or lexicalized: `ʔ-m-r`, `ġ-r`, `h-l-k`, `ḥ-r-š`, `n-s(-y)`, `q-n-ṣ`, `r-q-ṣ`, `s-r(-r)`, `š-ʔ-l`, `š-m-ʕ`, `š-m-d`/`ṣ-m-d`, `š-t-k`, `ṯ-n`; `ḏ-m-r` also occurs in onomastics.
- Denominative: `ṯ-m-n`, possibly `š-b-m`.
- Non-agentive/resultant: `n-p-l`, `ʔ-b-d`, `ʔ-ṯ-m`, `š-ʔ-r`, and context-dependent `n-š-ʔ`.

Chapter 6 explicitly debates or qualifies `ỉ/t/yštql`, `tqġ`, `ytʕn`, `tʕtd`, `tṯtny[n`, `yštd`, `tštr`, `tmtʕ`, and `twtḥ`. Preserve uncertainty and competing stem analyses for these forms.

## Semantic Review Questions

Ask in this order:

1. Is an animate Agent controlling the event?
2. Are Agent and Patient co-referential (direct reflexive)?
3. Are Agent and Recipient co-referential while an object remains (indirect reflexive)?
4. Is a second participant introduced by `ʕm` in a nonsymmetric reciprocal construction?
5. Does the Gt preserve transitivity but focus or intensify the Agent's affected action (autobenefactive)?
6. Is an inanimate Patient/Theme subject forcing a rare non-agentive reading?

If the consonantal form admits more than one answer, keep the morphological ambiguity rather than converting a discourse tendency into a deterministic parse.
